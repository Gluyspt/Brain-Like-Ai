# 22 — How humans relearn (and what that means for SPARC)

User brainstorm (2026-11-27): "humans take time even for relearning; if they didn't learn fully before, relearning almost gets worse; it takes effort, and real understanding makes recall easy." Checked against research:

| Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Relearning takes time | It is faster than first learning (**savings**) even when performance had dropped: [LearnLab](https://learnlab.org/research/wiki/index.php/Savings_in_re-learning). Overlearning improves retention with diminishing returns, sometimes not at all: [Krueger 1929](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1929-krueger.pdf) [abstract] | true, but faster than the first time |
| Partly learned → relearning gets worse | Temporary dips are real: **U-shaped learning** (went → goed → went) during reorganisation ([Marcus et al. 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1518508/); [Taatgen & Anderson 2002](https://www.ai.rug.nl/~niels/publications/Past-Tense-published.pdf)); **desirable difficulties**: effortful practice looks worse now, better later ([Bjork & Bjork 2011](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf); [2020](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2021/01/RABjorkELBjorkJARMAC2020ForPostingSingleSpaced.pdf)); **interference** between similar associations depends on their relative strength ([2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-010-0017-4); [2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328x/15/10/1332)). No evidence that relearning *ends* worse; one analysis argues savings do not depend on original learning strength ([2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-022-02172-3)) [abstract] | dips during relearning: yes; ending worse: not supported |
| Effort and understanding make recall easy | **Levels of processing**: meaning-based encoding is remembered better ([Craik & Lockhart 1972](https://psych205.stanford.edu/papers/CraikLockhart72.pdf)); elaboration doubled cued recall (Craik & Tulving 1975, [summary](https://www.simplypsychology.org/levelsofprocessing.html)); "storage strength" = how interconnected a memory is (Bjork) [abstract] | well supported |

## Implications for SPARC
1. SPARC's old relearning failure was **not** human-like: it got worse during practice and stayed worse after sleep (test: 76 → 68 → 62 %). Cause found 2026-11-27 (docs/iteration-log.md R4): homeostatic excitability drifting while awake; in brains this homeostasis is slow and largely sleep-dependent.
2. A **temporary dip during relearning is acceptable**, even human-like, if the outcome after consolidation is better. The relearning test should be judged **after sleep and after a delay**, not on the practice curve (benchmark change: SAV_after_sleep is the primary relearning score; a delayed check is to be added).
3. **"Understanding → easy recall"** is a testable prediction for stage 3: items embedded in a structure (maps, relations, number sense) should show larger savings than isolated items.
